Back-to-school readiness: grounding writing and literacy instruction in the science of reading

As summer begins to wind down, educators, tutors, and literacy interventionists face a familiar seasonal question: How do we structure our early autumn lessons to set students up for meaningful success from day one?

Transitioning back into academic routines can be daunting for students, especially those with language-based learning differences like dyslexia. When preparing your literacy block for the upcoming school year, the most impactful step you can take is anchoring your instruction in explicit, systematic, and structured literacy principles.

While much of the back-to-school conversation naturally centers on decoding, phonemic awareness, and phonics, explicit instruction in writing and composition is an equally important, yet often overlooked, pillar of foundational literacy achievement.

The science of reading and writing: what research tells us

The science of reading reminds us that reading and writing are reciprocal processes: two sides of the same cognitive coin. When students are taught how language is structured at the sentence level, their reading comprehension improves in tandem with their expressive language skills.

Dr. Louisa Moats, author of Speech to Print and lead developer of LETRS, has described what she saw when that instruction was missing. Looking at writing samples from students who could read well enough to score in the average range, she found they still could not produce a grammatical sentence:

They've gotten through fourth grade without anybody teaching them in any kind of systematic way how to handle simple syntax, complex syntax, and standard word usage.

Dr. Louisa Moats, interviewed for Children of the Code

When setting up a structured writing block, the goal for the back-to-school period isn't to overwhelm students with multi-paragraph essays right away. Instead, educators can lay a resilient foundation by explicitly teaching sentence structure, syntactic control, and morphological awareness from the start.

3 actionable steps to prepare your back-to-school writing block

Start at the sentence level. Before moving to extended writing tasks, assess and build students' mastery of complete sentences, run-ons, and fragments. Explicitly modeling sentence types (declarative, interrogative, exclamatory, imperative) and sentence expansion lowers cognitive load and builds stamina.

Integrate syntax and morphology daily. Connect decoding and phonics work directly to writing. Teach base words, prefixes, and suffixes as structural elements that inform both spelling accuracy and sentence function.

Use standardized, repeatable lesson templates. Rely on consistent, structured routines. Predictable frameworks allow students to focus their working memory on sentence construction rather than deciphering brand-new instructions every lesson.

Final thoughts for the new school year

Explicit composition training is often the "missing link" that helps struggling readers bridge the gap between word recognition and deep comprehension. By establishing predictable, evidence-based writing routines early in the term, educators provide students with the scaffolded support they need to build confidence and thrive all year long.

Here's to a structured and inspiring school year ahead.
